# Demo video script: Audubon Pond, Allen, Texas, at dusk (target about 3 minutes)

Two presenters: **Johnathan** (J) lives near the pond and tells why this matters. **Aryan** (A) walks through the app.
Swap any lines that fit the other person better. Filmed outdoors at the pond as it gets dark, with phone screen recordings cut in.
Lines in **[J]** must be Johnathan's own true words. Don't read them word for word; say them the way you'd tell a friend.
Every number below comes from the app's saved data for this neighborhood or from the sources at the end.

**The shape:** start inside the emergency, stop on the number that matters (nine minutes), then explain why we built this.
Keep the numbers few and let them land. Pauses are part of the script.

**Delivery notes**
- **The cold open only works if it's quiet and real.** No music under it. Don't overact: a calm "Hey, can you hear me?" is scarier than shouting.
- **Pauses are where the feeling lands.** After "Nine minutes." and after Johnathan's story, count two full seconds before speaking.
- **Johnathan's story is the heart of the video.** Short and specific beats long and general. Don't add anything that didn't happen; judges can tell, and the truth is enough.
- If you add music, start it at 1:50 ("So we built Heartline"), quietly, and cut it for the final silence.

## Safety for the act-out

- **Never press the call button for real.** Tapping "Call 911" dials 911. Show the button, don't tap it. Do that part in the screen recording with the call screen cut out, or film over the shoulder and stop before it dials.
- **Never do compressions on a real person**, even gently. Chest compressions can injure someone whose heart is beating. Use a pillow, a rolled jacket, or a CPR training manikin.
- Before filming in a public spot, give anyone nearby a heads-up that you're filming a pretend emergency, so nobody calls for real help.

## Filming at dusk

- **Light your faces, not the sky.** Face the sunset, or use a small light or a car's interior light. A bright sky behind dark faces looks like silhouettes.
- **With two people, one can film while the other talks** for the solo parts. Use a tripod or a propped-up phone for the shots with both of you.
- **Record the phone screens separately** with the phone's own screen recorder, then cut them in. Filming a phone screen outdoors at dusk will glare and flicker.
- **Wind and ducks:** use a wired or clip-on mic if you have one. Otherwise turn your back to the wind.
- **Turn the CPR beat volume down** a bit so it doesn't drown out your voice.
- **The app works with no signal around Audubon Pond.** The neighborhood's map data is saved in the app, so a weak signal at the pond is fine. If the status line says "showing saved results", that's expected.
- Sunset in Allen in late October is around 6:40–6:50 PM (check the exact time for your filming day). Start filming about 20 minutes before so the dark shots at the end are really dark.

## Sources for the numbers

- Larsen MP et al. Predicting survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a graphic model. *Ann Emerg Med* 1993;22(11):1652–1658. [doi:10.1016/S0196-0644(05)81302-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0196-0644(05)81302-2). Survival fell about 5.5 percentage points per minute with no treatment.
- CARES. *National Survival Report, 2013–2025, Non-Traumatic Etiology* (2026): 71.6% at home, 32.8% vs 10.2% survival ("about three times higher"), 2.1% bystander AED use.
- Wallace SK et al. Effect of time of day on prehospital care and outcomes after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. *Circulation* 2013;127(15):1591–1596. [doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.113.002058](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.113.002058). Night was 8 PM–8 AM; 30-day survival 8.56% vs 10.9%.
- Deakin CD et al. Underutilisation of public access defibrillation is related to retrieval distance and time-dependent availability. *Heart* 2018;104(16):1339–1343. [doi:10.1136/heartjnl-2018-312998](https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2018-312998). 34.3% of AEDs available at night.
- Note for Q&A: not every study finds a night effect. Vienna found none, crediting high bystander CPR rates at all hours (Schriefl C et al. *Resuscitation* 2019;142:61–68, [doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2019.07.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2019.07.009)). That supports the point: what matters is bystanders acting fast.
- Allen-wide count: `node scripts/count_aeds.mjs "Allen" "Texas" 104627` on 2026-10-20 found 0 mapped AEDs (see `docs/evidence-aed-counts.md`); population is the 2020 Census count.
- Neighborhood numbers: Heartline's saved OpenStreetMap data for Audubon Pond (`data/allen-audubon-pond.json`, made with `node scripts/snapshot.mjs`). "Open" for schools, churches and gyms uses typical hours for that kind of place, since their real AED hours aren't on the map.
